# Cardinal Trust Bank — Consumer Credit Notice Policy (working reference)

*Internal policy, Part 3. Fictional, authored for training use.*

**CT-1 — Scope.**
This policy covers unsecured personal loans, credit cards and overdraft lines
offered to consumers by Cardinal Trust Bank. Business credit is out of scope and
is governed by a separate policy.

**CT-2 — The seven-element template.**
Every consumer adverse-action notice is assembled from the approved template,
which carries seven elements: (E1) the specific principal reasons; (E2) the ECOA
anti-discrimination notice; (E3) the name and address of the federal enforcement
agency; (E4) the consumer reporting agency's name, address and toll-free number;
(E5) the statement that the agency did not make the decision; (E6) the right to a
free copy of the report within 60 days; (E7) the right to dispute inaccuracies.

**CT-3 — Disclosure text is fixed.**
The statutory disclosure paragraphs (E2 through E7) are **fixed template text**.
They are never drafted, paraphrased or regenerated per letter, by a person or by
an automated system. Only the reason statement (E1) and the applicant's details
vary between letters.

**CT-4 — Reasons come from the decision record.**
The reasons stated in a notice must be taken from the principal reasons recorded
by the underwriting engine for that application. No other reason may be added,
including a reason a reviewer believes to be true, and including information the
applicant volunteered in free text on their application.

**CT-5 — Protected characteristics.**
A notice must not refer, anywhere outside the standard ECOA notice paragraph, to
an applicant's race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age,
pregnancy or parental leave, or receipt of income from any public assistance
programme — even where the applicant raised the matter themselves.

**CT-6 — Federal enforcement agency.**
For Cardinal Trust Bank, the federal agency named in element E3 is the Bureau of
Consumer Financial Protection, 1700 G Street NW, Washington, DC 20552.

**CT-7 — Second review.**
Any notice generated by an automated drafting system must pass the deterministic
element check before despatch, and a sample of at least 5% must be read by a
credit officer each week. A notice that fails the element check is never sent; it
is returned for regeneration.

**CT-8 — Retention.**
Copies of consumer adverse-action notices are retained for 25 months from the date
of notification, in line with the Regulation B requirement in RB-11.

**CT-9 — Turnaround target.**
Cardinal Trust's internal service target is to despatch the notice within **10
business days** of the decision. This is an internal target only and does not
displace the 30-day regulatory deadline in RB-3.
