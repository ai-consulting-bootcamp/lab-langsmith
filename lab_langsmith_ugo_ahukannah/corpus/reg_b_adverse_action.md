# Regulation B — Adverse Action Notification (working reference)

*Cardinal Trust Bank compliance handbook, Part 1. Authored for training use.*

**RB-1 — Definition of adverse action.**
Adverse action means a refusal to grant credit in substantially the amount or on
substantially the terms requested in an application, unless the creditor makes a
counteroffer and the applicant uses or expressly accepts the credit offered. It
also covers a termination of an account, or an unfavourable change in the terms of
an account, that does not affect all or substantially all of a class of the
creditor's accounts.

**RB-2 — What is not adverse action.**
A change in the terms of an account expressly agreed to by the applicant is not
adverse action. Nor is a refusal to extend credit because doing so would exceed a
limit set under a governmental credit programme, nor a refusal because the
creditor does not offer the type of credit or credit plan requested.

**RB-3 — Timing: completed applications.**
The creditor must notify the applicant of action taken within **30 days** after
receiving a **completed** application. The 30-day clock starts when the
application is complete, not when it was first received.

**RB-4 — Timing: incomplete applications.**
Where an application is incomplete, the creditor must within 30 days either send a
notice of incompleteness specifying the information needed and a reasonable period
to provide it, or send an adverse action notice. A notice of incompleteness must
state that failure to supply the information will mean no further consideration is
given to the application.

**RB-5 — Timing: counteroffers.**
Where the creditor makes a counteroffer, it must notify the applicant within 30
days. If the applicant does not expressly accept or use the credit offered within
**90 days** of the counteroffer, the creditor must then send an adverse action
notice.

**RB-6 — Timing: existing accounts.**
For adverse action taken on an existing account, notification must be given within
**30 days** after taking the action.

**RB-7 — Required content of the notice.**
An adverse action notice must contain: a statement of the action taken; the name
and address of the creditor; the ECOA anti-discrimination notice; the name and
address of the federal agency that administers compliance for that creditor; and
**either** a statement of the specific principal reasons for the action **or** a
disclosure of the applicant's right to obtain those reasons within 30 days of
requesting them, together with the name, address and telephone number of the
person who can provide them.

**RB-8 — Specificity of reasons.**
Reasons given must be specific and must indicate the principal reasons for the
adverse action. A statement that the applicant failed to achieve a qualifying
score on a credit scoring system is not sufficient on its own; the notice must
disclose the principal factors that contributed to the score. Reasons such as
"insufficient information" or "you do not meet our minimum standards" are too
vague to satisfy this requirement. A creditor need not describe how or why a
factor was weighted, and need not list more than four principal reasons.

**RB-9 — Multiple applicants.**
Where there is more than one applicant, the notice need only be given to one of
them, but it must go to the primary applicant where one is readily apparent.

**RB-10 — Business credit.**
Different notification rules apply to business credit. Where the applicant's gross
revenues exceeded $1 million in the preceding fiscal year, the creditor may notify
orally or in writing, and need only provide the statement of reasons on the
applicant's written request.

**RB-11 — Record retention.**
For applications for consumer credit, the creditor must retain the application and
all written or recorded information connected with it, including a copy of the
adverse action notice, for **25 months** after the date the applicant is notified.
