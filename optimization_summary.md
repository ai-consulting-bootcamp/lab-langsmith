# Cost & Performance Optimization Summary

The cost-performance comparison showed that `gpt-4.1` substantially outperformed the `gpt-4o-mini` baseline on the same 15-example clinical-trial eligibility dataset, increasing mean criterion-level label accuracy from **0.473 to 0.867** and the fully correct response rate from **40% to 80%**. `gpt-4.1` improved seven examples, performed worse on none, and also showed lower mean latency in this experiment (1.23 s vs. 1.80 s), but its total target-model cost was approximately **14.8× higher** ($0.0345 vs. $0.00234), with cost per fully correct case about **7.4× higher**. For low-cost, high-volume screening where errors are acceptable and human review is guaranteed, `gpt-4o-mini` provides the better budget option; for clinical decision-support scenarios where criterion-level accuracy is more important than marginal API cost, `gpt-4.1` is the stronger choice. The remaining shared failures on ambiguous-evidence and not-applicable cases suggest that further gains may require prompt or label-ontology refinement rather than model scaling alone.
